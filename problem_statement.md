Allotted Project: Probing the Latent Space of Video Diffusion U-Nets: Disentangling Content vs. Motion

Project Description: Video diffusion U-Nets interleave spatial layers (appearance) and temporal layers (motion). Team will use activation-patching / cross-attention visualization techniques to probe which layers/timesteps control appearance vs. motion in a small open video diffusion model and demonstrate a simple edit (e.g., keep motion, change appearance) enabled by this understanding. For this project, "appearance/content" is defined as the static visual characteristics of a frame like object identity, colour, texture, and scene layout, i.e. what is depicted — while "motion" is defined as the temporal dynamics across frames which is how objects/pixels move, deform, or change position over time; independent of what is depicted. For example, in a generated clip of "a golden retriever running across a beach," the content is the dog's identity, fur colour, and the beach/sky background, whereas the motion is the running gait and the trajectory the dog follows across the frame; a model that has correctly disentangled the two should be able to keep the exact same running motion while changing the content to, say, a cat or a horse, or conversely keep the same dog while changing its motion to walking or jumping.

Expected Deliverables: 1. Video Diffusion Model Setup & Probing: Select and configure a small open video diffusion model and develop tools to extract and analyze U-Net activations and cross-attention maps across layers and diffusion timesteps. 2. Content Motion Representation Analysis: Systematically probe the model to identify which spatial layers, temporal layers and diffusion timesteps primarily encode static content and appearance versus temporal motion information. 3. Activation Patching & Intervention Framework: Develop an activation-patching or intervention mechanism that selectively transfers content and motion related representations between video samples to test the discovered disentanglement. 4. Content–Motion Disentanglement Demonstration: Demonstrate controlled editing such as preserving the original motion while changing object appearance and conversely preserving content while modifying the motion.
