# Pinning down where AnimateDiff computes motion

Experiment 2 should test one claim from several directions: early decoder motion modules carry *generated* motion, not prompt-word identity, and the model *uses* that signal. Each method in the plan is published and comes with an explicit null. The design has five strands. First, it repeats Experiment 1 on a 2×2 grid of base model {SD1.5, epiCRealism} × motion adapter {v1-5-2, v1-5-3}. All four cells use the same prompts and the same CPU-drawn noise, so every comparison is a *paired* contrast that can be tested with subject-level sign flips. Second, it replaces word-identity swaps with a seven-rung content ladder and analyses each layer's dose-response slope against measured CLIP distance. Third, it turns motion from a verb label into a measured quantity: RAFT flow, with the camera motion removed, inside a subject mask built from cross-attention. That flow enters RSA regressions, within-prompt decoding and pair validation. Fourth, it re-reads all 95 hooked modules (92 on v1-5-3) × 25 steps through debiased CKA, RSA, ANOVA variance fractions, control-task probes and MDL, with cluster-mass and sign-flip statistics. Fifth, it upgrades "encodes" to "uses" only when presence, causal sufficiency, causal necessity and a double dissociation all hold. A LoRA test then puts the localisation claim at risk: probe-selected layers against 19 parameter-matched random layer subsets, scored with Motion Fidelity Score. Two changes matter most, because Experiment 1's weak points were word-level labels and underpowered one-way patching. Motion becomes measured rather than named. Patching is run in both directions on about 25 pairs per claim; Experiment 1's 12 pairs gave a CI that only just cleared zero. The plan costs about 90 of the roughly 120 Kaggle GPU-hours available before the 23–25 Nov 2026 novelty assessment. One caveat runs through everything: the decision rule, the per-frame DAAM extension, the flow-mask pipeline and the use of LEACE and attribution patching inside a video U-Net are the researchers' own synthesis. No published study has done them for AnimateDiff.

## Three weaknesses in Experiment 1 set the agenda

Experiment 1 found content dominant almost everywhere. The only motion-leaning site was the `up_blocks.0` motion module (8×8) in denoising steps 0–2. H1 was falsified narrowly: it predicted `up_blocks.1`. H2 survived, with a changepoint at step 2. H3 was falsified (f_stat 0.65, no sign flips). Linear verb probes reached about 35–50% against a 25% chance level. Component patches were positive on the median but had CIs that included zero. Temporal-attention injection was the best motion intervention (median gain +0.105, CI [0.001, 0.204]). Three motion LoRAs showed nothing. Read against the literature, three weaknesses explain much of this, and each sets a requirement for Experiment 2.

**First, "motion" was a word, and SD1.5 morphs.** The dynamic part of an activation is the frame-mean-removed residual. On a base that morphs, that residual contains appearance drift as well as movement. A verb probe can also succeed because every module downstream of cross-attention sees the verb token. Pimentel et al. make this precise. A label that is a deterministic function of the input is bounded by the data-processing inequality, so "how much information" is uninformative ([Pimentel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03061)). Experiment 2 therefore needs a measured motion variable that varies *within* a prompt.

**Second, the coarse grid may have missed the literature's motion site.** Every training-free motion-feature method points to the 16×16 decoder level. MOFT takes features "after upper block 1" ([MOFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14864v3)). MotionClone guides temporal attention in `up_block.1` and found it best in ablation ([MotionClone](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05338v6)). DMT uses the second decoder block ([DMT](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17009)). PnP injects at `up_blocks[1].resnets[1]` ([pnp-diffusers](https://github.com/MichalGeyer/pnp-diffusers/blob/main/pnp_utils.py)), and DIFT's semantic default is `up_ft_index=1` ([DIFT](https://github.com/Tsingularity/dift)). Experiment 1's 16 coordinates sampled one temporal module per block, while `up_blocks.1` has three, so H1's "narrow" falsification needs a sub-module re-test. MOFT's channel ablation adds a sharper warning. Using 100% of channels scored 67.5 on motion faithfulness, against 84.0 for the PCA-selected top 4% ([MOFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14864v3)). Full-width distances, as used in Experiment 1, may dilute a sparse motion code.

**Third, the causal tests were underpowered and one-directional.** Treat the bootstrap CI of the injection result as roughly normal. Then a half-width of about 0.10 on 12 pairs implies a per-pair SD of about $0.10\sqrt{12}/1.96 \approx 0.18$. Detecting a 0.10 effect with 80% power at two-sided α = 0.05 then needs $n \approx ((1.96+0.84)\times0.18/0.10)^2 \approx 25$ pairs. This is the researchers' own approximate calculation, not a published figure. Patching in only one direction also tests sufficiency but never necessity. Heimersheim and Nanda show the two directions disagree in predictable ways under AND-like and OR-like redundancy ([Heimersheim & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15255)).

## The 2×2 grid separates image prior from motion prior only as paired contrasts

The grid holds the sampler fixed across cells: 16 frames, 512², 25 DDIM steps (η = 0), CFG 7.5, fp16, the same CPU-drawn noise per seed. It crosses the spatial weights (SD1.5 vs `emilianJR/epiCRealism`) with the temporal weights (`guoyww/animatediff-motion-adapter-v1-5-2` vs `-v1-5-3`). The diffusers docs pair both adapters with community fine-tunes such as epiCRealism. They also require `beta_schedule="linear"`, `clip_sample=False`, `timestep_spacing="linspace"` and `steps_offset=1`, because the checkpoints are "sensitive to the beta schedule" ([diffusers AnimateDiff docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/api/pipelines/animatediff)). The v1-5-3 model card demonstrates an Euler-ancestral scheduler ([v1-5-3 card](https://huggingface.co/guoyww/animatediff-motion-adapter-v1-5-3)). Experiment 2 should keep DDIM with η = 0 in every cell anyway. An ancestral sampler injects fresh noise at every step, which would break lockstep pairing and the trajectory caching used later for patching.

The architectural difference is concrete. v1-5-2 sets `use_motion_mid_block: true` ([v1-5-2 config](https://huggingface.co/guoyww/animatediff-motion-adapter-v1-5-2/raw/main/config.json)). v1-5-3 sets it to `false` and otherwise matches: two motion layers per block, 8 heads, `max_seq_length` 32, channels [320, 640, 1280, 1280] ([v1-5-3 config](https://huggingface.co/guoyww/animatediff-motion-adapter-v1-5-3/raw/main/config.json)). Down blocks get `motion_layers_per_block` modules and up blocks one more ([diffusers unet_motion_model.py](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_motion_model.py)). That gives 4×2 + 1 + 4×3 = 21 motion modules on v1-5-2 and 20 on v1-5-3. Experiment 1 hooked 95 modules: 21 motion modules, 42 temporal attentions and 32 spatial attentions. On v1-5-3 this becomes, by the researchers' arithmetic, 20 + 40 + 32 = **92 modules**, or 2,300 module-step cells against 2,375. Confirm the count by enumerating `unet.named_modules()`. Hook lists must be built dynamically, and cells should be reported by (block, position) rather than by absolute index, so the 92 shared modules can be compared one to one.

| Comparison | Held fixed | Changed | What it can show | What it cannot show |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base swap at v1-5-2 (SD1.5→epiC) | Motion weights | Spatial weights / image prior | Whether the motion-leaning site and its step window depend on the features *fed into* the motion modules, and whether SD1.5 morphing inflated "dynamic" distances | Which property of the fine-tune (data, aesthetics, VAE pairing) is responsible; one base is one sample of "better bases" |
| Base swap at v1-5-3 | Motion weights | Spatial weights | Same, under the newer motion prior | Same |
| Adapter swap at SD1.5 (v1-5-2→v1-5-3) | Spatial weights | Motion weights *and* architecture (no mid motion module) | Whether localisation is a property of the motion prior | Whether a difference comes from the training recipe or from removing the mid-block module; v3's domain adapter is not loaded (see below) |
| Adapter swap at epiC | Spatial weights | Motion weights | Same, on a non-morphing base | Same |
| Interaction (difference of differences) | — | Both | Whether the base effect depends on the adapter | Generalisation to other checkpoints (n = 1 per level) |
| Diagonal (SD1.5×v1-5-2 vs epiC×v1-5-3) | — | Both | Headline "does it replicate on a better model" | Anything causal, because both factors change |

One confound sits in the v1-5-3 column. The v3 release ships a Domain-Adapter LoRA, trained to absorb training-video artefacts ([AnimateDiff README](https://github.com/guoyww/AnimateDiff)). The notes found no official diffusers route for loading it ([diffusers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/api/pipelines/animatediff)). The documented path, which the v1-5-3 card follows, runs the motion module without it. Experiment 2 therefore tests "v1-5-3 as diffusers ships it", not the full v3 system. That v1-5-3 is the diffusers form of the v3 motion module is an inference the notes did not confirm.

Because every cell shares prompts and noise, the right statistic is a within-subject cell difference. For subject $s$, module-step cell $(m,t)$ and model cells $a, b$, define $\Delta_s(m,t) = \bar S_s^{(a)}(m,t) - \bar S_s^{(b)}(m,t)$. Test it with an exact sign flip over the 15 subjects ($2^{15} = 32{,}768$ patterns), applying the same flip to a subject's whole map so that spatial and temporal dependence is preserved. Use max-statistic or cluster-mass correction across the map ([Maris & Oostenveld](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2007.03.024)). Experiment 1 also supplies a *pre-registered region*: `up_blocks.0` motion modules, steps 0–2. Replication in a new cell is a single one-sided contrast on that region and needs no map-wide correction. The map-wide search is reported separately as exploratory. This split also handles double-dipping: discovery happened on the SD1.5×v1-5-2 data, and the three new cells are the confirmation set.

A quality gate runs before the full sweep. It uses VBench-style metrics for each cell. Subject Consistency is DINO-feature similarity between frame t and both frame 1 and frame t−1 ([VBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17982)). Dynamic Degree uses RAFT. A frame pair moves if the mean of its top 5% flow magnitudes exceeds 6.0 × (short side/256), which is 12 px at 512. A video is dynamic if at least round(4 × count/16) pairs move ([VBench dynamic_degree.py](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench/blob/master/vbench/dynamic_degree.py)). A variant with high subject consistency and non-trivial dynamic degree really moves; one that only flickers will not pass. FreeInit improves temporal consistency (DINO 85.24 → 92.01 on UCF-101) ([FreeInit](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07537v2)), but it changes the initial-noise distribution and so confounds per-step probing. Leave it out of Experiment 2.

## A seven-rung content ladder turns word swaps into dose-response curves

Experiment 1's content pairs swapped whole subjects (dog→horse, quadruped→human). That gives one, maximal dose. A ladder of graded swaps asks a sharper question: does a layer's activation change scale with *how much* content changed, and does its response profile match published layer roles? P+ found that "the coarse layers primarily affect the structure of the image" while "the fine layers determine the color appearance" ([P+](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09522v3)). That yields a falsifiable prior. Colour and texture rungs should load relatively more on 32×32 and 64×64 layers. Breed and species rungs should load on 8×8 and 16×16 layers.

| Rung | Swap (example) | Expected locus of change | Prior |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (null) | "a dog" → "one dog" (determiner/punctuation) | Text-perturbation floor $d_0$ | Design control |
| 1 colour | "a black dog" → "a white dog" | Subject surface | P+: fine layers |
| 2 coat/texture | "short-haired" → "long-haired / spotted" | Subject surface + silhouette edge | P+: fine layers |
| 3 breed within family | labrador → dalmatian; tabby → siamese; arabian → clydesdale | Shape + surface | Intermediate |
| 4 species within family | dog → wolf; house cat → lynx; horse → zebra | Shape/structure | P+: coarse layers |
| 5 scene | "on a beach" → "in a snowy forest" | Background, global layout | Exploratory |
| 6 lighting | "in daylight" → "at golden-hour sunset" / "at night" | Global photometry | Exploratory (no source) |

The ladder has two branches. Rungs 1–4 change the *subject*; rungs 5–6 change *context*. They should be analysed as separate dose axes, not one ordinal scale. Rung labels serve only for stratification and design balance. The analysis regresses on *measured* content change, so overlapping rungs do no harm. A ladder-validity check runs first: Spearman ρ between rung index and measured Δc within the subject branch should be positive. If two rungs overlap in Δc, merge them.

The ladder runs on the primary model chosen from the 2×2 (expected to be epiCRealism). It uses 8 base quadrupeds drawn from at least 4 families (canid, felid, equid, bovid), 2 verbs (the two best rendered in the pilot), 2 seeds and 7 variants per base (6 rungs plus the null rung). That is 32 base clips and 224 pairs. If the lockstep sampler can reuse the base run, the cost is 256 clip generations. If it must regenerate the base for every pair, the cost is 448. Eight subjects is the minimum that permits subject-level inference. With $2^8 = 256$ sign patterns the smallest two-sided p is 2/256 ≈ 0.008. Four families alone would floor at 2/16 = 0.125 and could never reach significance.

**Dose and response.** For pair $p$ with clips A and B, define the measured content change as the frame-averaged CLIP image-embedding distance:

$$\Delta c(p) = 1 - \frac{1}{F}\sum_{f=1}^{F}\cos\big(e(x_A^f),\, e(x_B^f)\big)$$

Report a DINO-based version as a structure-sensitive alternative. The measured motion change $\Delta f(p)$ is the flow distance defined in the next section. The layer response is Experiment 1's exact cosine distance on full flattened activations, $d^{\pi}_{m,t}(p) = 1-\cos(h^{m,t}_A, h^{m,t}_B)$ for $\pi \in$ {static, dynamic}, normalised by the null-rung floor, $\tilde d = d/\bar d_0$. The per-subject model is:

$$\tilde d^{\pi}_{m,t}(p) = \alpha_{s,m,t} + \beta_{s,m,t}\,\Delta c(p) + \gamma_{s,m,t}\,\Delta f(p) + \varepsilon_p$$

It is fitted by OLS on each subject's 28 pairs. The group statistic is the mean of $\beta_{s,m,t}$ over subjects, tested by sign flip with max-statistic FWER over the map. $\beta$ is the layer's content sensitivity. $\gamma$ is how much a *content* swap moves the layer through its side effect on generated motion. A large $\gamma$ in a motion module during content swaps marks that module as motion-tracking. A large $\beta$ in the *dynamic* part of a motion module, with $\gamma$ partialled out, marks appearance leaking into the temporal pathway.

**Shape of the curve.** Fitting $\log \tilde d = a + b\log\Delta c$ gives an elasticity $b$. Values $b<1$ mark a layer that responds strongly even to small changes such as colour, an "appearance" layer. Values $b>1$ mark a layer that responds only to large categorical changes such as species, a "semantic/structural" layer. The P+ prior then becomes an interaction test: elasticity should fall with resolution, or equivalently the colour-to-species response ratio $\tilde d(\text{rung 1})/\tilde d(\text{rung 4})$ should rise from 8×8 to 64×64. Test this as a (rung × resolution) contrast with subject-level sign flips. This prediction is the researchers' derivation from P+, which studied image cross-attention conditioning rather than activation distances. P+ itself warns that "the disentanglement among the layers of U-net is not perfect" ([P+](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09522v3)). The regression, elasticity and ratio contrasts are not taken from a published dose-response protocol for diffusion models; none was found.

The ladder also extends naturally to motion: "walking slowly" → "walking" → "jogging" → "running", analysed with the same equations with the roles of $\Delta c$ and $\Delta f$ swapped. It is optional and is not in the budget below.

## Optical flow turns "motion" from a word into a measured quantity

**Extracting flow.** Use torchvision's `raft_large` with `Raft_Large_Weights.DEFAULT` (C_T_SKHT_V2: 5.26M parameters, Sintel-test EPE 1.819 clean / 3.067 final, inputs scaled to [−1, 1], H and W divisible by 8) ([torchvision raft_large](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/main/models/generated/torchvision.models.optical_flow.raft_large.html)). Take the last of the 12 iterative predictions, which is the most accurate ([torchvision tutorial](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/main/auto_examples/others/plot_optical_flow.html); [RAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12039v2)). Each 16-frame clip gives 15 flow fields $w_f(\mathbf x)$. The notes did not find a source comparing RAFT with Farneback on generated video. Preferring RAFT is a judgement call; keep Farneback only as a sanity check.

**Removing camera motion and isolating the subject.** Let $M_f$ be the binary subject mask for frame $f$ from per-frame cross-attention (next-but-one section), dilated a few pixels. Background points $\mathbf x \notin M_f$ give a robust global fit. Either take the median background flow $\bar{\mathbf c}_f$, or fit a RANSAC homography or affine map $H_f$ to correspondences $(\mathbf x, \mathbf x + w_f(\mathbf x))$, which also captures pan, zoom and rotation. Camera flow is $c_f(\mathbf x) = H_f(\mathbf x) - \mathbf x$ and object flow is $o_f(\mathbf x) = w_f(\mathbf x) - c_f(\mathbf x)$. Four per-clip summaries follow: camera magnitude $\overline{\|c_f\|}$, object speed $\overline{\|o_f\|}_{M_f}$, dominant object direction, and a **morph index** $\overline{\|o_f\|}_{\neg M_f}$. Flicker and morphing appear as incoherent residual flow off the subject. This pipeline is the notes' proposal. No published paper was found that combines cross-attention subject masks with flow to measure object-only motion in AnimateDiff.

**A flow descriptor and a flow RDM.** Pool $o_f$ inside the mask to the feature grids that matter: 8×8 for `up_blocks.0` and 16×16 for `up_blocks.1`. Concatenate over $f$ to get $\Phi(x)\in\mathbb R^{15\cdot G^2\cdot 2}$, and set $D_{\text{flow}}(i,j) = 1-\cos(\Phi_i,\Phi_j)$. Baherwani et al. measured motion preservation with exactly this kind of cosine similarity on normalised Lucas–Kanade flow ([Baherwani et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22175)). Yatim et al. argue that raw flow-field comparison is "unsuitable" when structure changes, which is why they built the tracklet-based Motion Fidelity Score ([DMT/MFS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17009)). The flow RDM is therefore fine for same-structure pairs, such as verb swaps on one subject. Across species, where the subject's shape changes, MFS or orientation-histogram descriptors are the safer choice. The notes found no published flow RDM for gait or verbs in generated video, so the descriptor is a design choice and should be reported with a sensitivity analysis.

**RSA multiple regression with variance partitioning.** For each $(m,t)$, compute the layer RDM $D_L$ over clips *within a seed*. Clips that share a seed share their initial noise, so cross-seed pairs mix noise identity into every distance. Fit on the lower triangle, rank-transformed:

$$\operatorname{vec}(D_L) = \beta_0 + \beta_c\operatorname{vec}(D_{\text{CLIP}}) + \beta_m\operatorname{vec}(D_{\text{flow}}) + \beta_s\operatorname{vec}(D_{\text{scene}}) + \beta_x\operatorname{vec}(D_{\text{text}}) + \varepsilon$$

$D_{\text{CLIP}}$ comes from mean-frame CLIP image embeddings. $D_{\text{scene}}$ is categorical. $D_{\text{text}}$ is the distance between prompt text embeddings, a nuisance regressor for prompt-token identity. The approach follows RSA ([Kriegeskorte 2008](https://doi.org/10.3389/neuro.06.004.2008); [Nili et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003553)) and its variance-partitioning use in [Groen et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.32962); the notes flag Groen's method detail as recalled, not re-read. With content $c$ and motion $m$ as the predictors of interest, the commonality identities are:

$$U_c = R^2_{cm} - R^2_m,\qquad U_m = R^2_{cm} - R^2_c,\qquad C_{cm} = R^2_c + R^2_m - R^2_{cm}$$

With the nuisance set $z$ (scene, text) always included, replace each $R^2_\cdot$ by $R^2_{\cdot z}$. The shared term $C_{cm}$ can go negative under suppression. It is expected to be substantial, because subject size and body shape change flow. That overlap is exactly why partitioning is needed. A layer "geometrically tracks motion" at $(m,t)$ when $U_m>0$. These are standard identities written from definitions, not transcribed from a source.

Inference must not use parametric regression p-values, because RDM entries are dependent. Permute condition labels (rows and columns together) within seed, refit, and take the maximum over layers for FWER ([Nili et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003553)). The seed replicates give a noise ceiling. The lower bound is one seed's RDM correlated with the mean of the others; the upper bound uses the mean of all seeds. One caveat: CLIP image embeddings carry pose and action, so $D_{\text{CLIP}}$ is not pure content. Report its correlation with a categorical same-verb RDM, and rerun with categorical subject RDMs as the content model.

**Flow-target decoding that the prompt cannot explain.** The Pimentel argument says any layer that sees the text can decode the verb. The way out is to decode the part of motion that varies *within* a prompt across seeds. With 3 seeds, each prompt contributes 2 degrees of freedom. Residualise both sides:

$$\tilde y_{p,r} = y_{p,r} - \bar y_{p,\cdot}, \qquad \tilde x_{p,r} = x_{p,r} - \bar x_{p,\cdot}$$

Here $y$ is a flow target: camera vector, object speed, or the pooled object field $\Phi$. $x$ is the layer's dynamic-part sketch. Fit ridge regression $\hat W = \arg\min_W \|\tilde Y - \tilde X W\|^2 + \lambda\|W\|^2$ with leave-one-subject-out cross-validation, and report $R^2_{\text{CV}}$. Because seed noise drives both the activations and the final motion, the right control representation is the step-$t$ latent $z_t$ itself. The reported quantity is a gain in the spirit of Pimentel et al.'s $G = I(T;R) - I(T;c(R))$ ([Pimentel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03061)):

$$G_{m,t} = R^2_{\text{CV}}(\tilde x^{m,t}) - R^2_{\text{CV}}(\tilde z_t)$$

$G_{m,t}>0$ means the layer makes the *eventual* motion more linearly accessible than the raw noisy latent does. Plotted over $t$, the step where $G$ first rises gives a "motion commitment" time to compare with Experiment 1's changepoint. The MOFT observation that motion features are spatially aligned with the actual motion ([MOFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14864v3)) justifies decoding the pooled field at the feature resolution rather than only a clip-level scalar. The residualisation and latent-control construction are the researchers' synthesis.

**Using flow to validate pairs.** A one-word content swap should leave motion roughly fixed. A verb swap should change it. For every pair compute $\Delta f$ and compare it with the null-rung distribution. Content pairs above that distribution's 95th percentile have "motion spill". Verb pairs that sit inside the null distribution are verbs that did not render; VBench Dynamic Degree flags clips that do not move at all ([VBench dynamic_degree.py](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench/blob/master/vbench/dynamic_degree.py)). Excluding pairs on $\Delta f$ conditions on a post-treatment variable and can bias slopes. The primary analysis therefore keeps all pairs with $\Delta f$ as a covariate (the $\gamma$ term above), and exclusion is reported only as a sensitivity analysis.

## The refined pass reads 2,375 cells through five correlational lenses

### Where to look: layers, steps, channels and heads

All 95 modules (92 on v1-5-3) × 25 steps are sketched, which gives the map. Analysis effort is weighted by priors. `up_blocks.1` should be analysed at full sub-module granularity: three motion modules, both temporal attentions inside each, the three spatial transformers, and the three ResNets. ResNets are not among the 95 hooks today, but PnP's injection site is `up_blocks[1].resnets[1]` ([pnp-diffusers](https://github.com/MichalGeyer/pnp-diffusers/blob/main/pnp_utils.py)). Basu et al. also found that in SD v1.4 "the cross-attention layers are not causally important states", with knowledge spread over ResNet and self-attention blocks ([Basu et al. ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13730)). Add at least the three `up_blocks.1` ResNet outputs.

**Steps.** Under linspace spacing, step $s$ corresponds to $t \approx 999 - 41.6s$. Experiment 1's steps 0–2 are therefore $t \approx 999$–916. Baherwani et al.'s motion/appearance boundary at τ = 700 of 1000 for ModelScope ([Baherwani et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22175)) falls near step 7. MotionClone's extraction point $t_\alpha = 400$, where 800 "loses substantial motion information" ([MotionClone](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05338v6)), falls near step 14. Analyse steps 0–14 densely and treat 15–24 as the appearance-refinement control band. Hooks capture both CFG halves. Probe the conditional half as primary, since the unconditional half carries no prompt information.

**Channels.** Follow MOFT. Run PCA on frame-mean-removed features from clips with known motion direction (prompted pans or flow labels), rank channels by their loading on PC1, and keep the top 4% ([MOFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14864v3)). To avoid circularity, select channels on one seed and evaluate on another.

**Heads.** Each motion module has 8 heads ([v1-5-2 config](https://huggingface.co/guoyww/animatediff-motion-adapter-v1-5-2/raw/main/config.json)) and produces per-location $F\times F$ attention matrices $a_{ij}$. Three summaries are useful. Entropy is $H = -\sum_j a_{ij}\log a_{ij}$. Off-diagonal mass is $1-\frac1F\sum_i a_{ii}$. The top-1 index is MotionClone's sparse motion representation ([MotionClone](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05338v6)). Correlating each head's top-1 temporal offset with local flow direction tests head specialisation. Work on video DiTs reports temporal heads aligned with optical flow, but the notes saw it only in search snippets ([arXiv 2607.11081](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11081v1)). The notes found no head-level study of AnimateDiff motion modules.

### How to compare layers

**Debiased CKA, never biased CKA, at this sample size.** Linear CKA is $\text{HSIC}(K,L)/\sqrt{\text{HSIC}(K,K)\,\text{HSIC}(L,L)}$ with $\text{HSIC} = \operatorname{tr}(KHLH)/(n-1)^2$ ([Kornblith et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)). In the "low-data high-dimensionality" regime, biased CKA is high even for random matrices ([Murphy et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01012)). The research team's own simulation, which is not a published result, makes this concrete for this design. Two *independent* Gaussian matrices give biased CKA of 0.896 at n = 60, p = 512 and 0.809 at n = 120. Debiased CKA stays within ±0.004 of zero. Use the unbiased HSIC with $\tilde K$ (diagonal zeroed):

$$\text{HSIC}_u(K,L) = \frac{1}{n(n-3)}\left[\operatorname{tr}(\tilde K\tilde L) + \frac{\mathbf 1^\top\tilde K\mathbf 1\;\mathbf 1^\top\tilde L\mathbf 1}{(n-1)(n-2)} - \frac{2}{n-2}\mathbf 1^\top\tilde K\tilde L\mathbf 1\right]$$

Set dCKA $= \text{HSIC}_u(K,L)/\sqrt{\text{HSIC}_u(K,K)\,\text{HSIC}_u(L,L)}$ and report negative values as they are. The formula's attribution to Song et al. 2012 is unverified; the simulation is its only check. CCA and SVCCA are invariant to invertible linear maps and so become degenerate when width ≥ n ([Kornblith et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00414)). Leave them out. dCKA has three uses here. Within a cell, it gives module × module similarity at each step. Across steps, it shows when a module's geometry stops changing. Across 2×2 cells for the same module, it is valid because the samples are paired by prompt and noise, and it shows whether epiCRealism's `up_blocks.0` resembles SD1.5's. Ding et al. caution that CKA can miss functionally relevant differences ([Ding et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01661)). This is one reason causal tests are mandatory.

**ANOVA variance fractions are the clearest "how much is content vs motion" number.** The design is balanced, so Kobak et al.'s marginalisation is an exact orthogonal decomposition ([Kobak et al.](https://elifesciences.org/articles/10989)). Write clip activations as $x_{s,v,c,r,f}$: subject, verb, scene, seed, frame. Main effects are marginal means minus the grand mean, for example $X_V(v) = \langle x\rangle_{s c r f}(v) - \bar x$. Interactions subtract lower-order terms, and

$$\text{frac}_\phi = \frac{\sum\|X_\phi\|^2}{\sum\|x-\bar x\|^2},\qquad \sum_\phi \text{frac}_\phi = 1$$

Experiment 1's static/dynamic split is exactly {terms without F} vs {terms with F}. The ANOVA refines it. Content-static is $S + C + S{\times}C$. Motion is $V{\times}F + S{\times}V{\times}F + \dots$. Static-verb $V$ is pose carried in the frame mean. Seed terms measure how much the shared noise dominates each step. Seed is a crossed random factor with 3 levels, so treat its interactions as the error term. The small-replicate bias correction $\text{frac}^{\text{corr}}_\phi = (SS_\phi - df_\phi\,MS_{\text{res}})/(SS_{\text{tot}} + MS_{\text{res}})$ is an ω²-style correction written from first principles; no source verified it. Full dPCA decoder axes ([Kobak et al.](https://elifesciences.org/articles/10989)) are optional and useful mainly for visualising a verb axis. DCI and MIG add little. They assume axis-aligned codes, which a count sketch destroys, and Locatello et al. question disentanglement metrics generally ([Locatello et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12359)).

**Probes with control tasks and restricted permutations.** Hewitt-selectivity is $\text{Acc}_{\text{task}} - \text{Acc}_{\text{control}}$. The control assigns each *type* a random label drawn from the task's marginal ([Hewitt & Liang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368)). The current implementation matches the paper's selective settings: type = (subject, verb) per seed, MLP with hidden size 10 and L2 = 1.0. Hewitt and Liang found that constraining hidden dimension is "an effective way to encourage selectivity", that dropout is not, and that a linear probe on ELMo1 had selectivity 26.0 against 4.5 for a default MLP ([Hewitt & Liang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368)). Draw at least 20 control mappings and report their mean and SD. At about 60 test clips, the binomial SE of selectivity is about 0.09, so layer differences below roughly 0.15–0.2 mean nothing without the permutation machinery. Use Ojala–Garriga Test 1 ([Ojala & Garriga](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/ojala10a.html)) with blocked permutations: verbs permuted within (subject, seed), with the same permutation in both scenes. Use $p = (b+1)/(B+1)$ ([Phipson & Smyth](https://doi.org/10.2202/1544-6115.1585); the formula is recalled, not re-read). The existing max-over-map FWER construction is the right one.

**MDL as a small-n accessibility measure.** The online code is

$$L_{\text{online}} = t_1\log_2K - \sum_{i=1}^{S-1}\log_2 p_{\theta_i}\big(y_{t_i+1:t_{i+1}}\mid x_{t_i+1:t_{i+1}}\big)$$

with compression $n\log_2K/L_{\text{online}}$ ([Voita & Titov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)). Voita and Titov's 0.1% first block is under one clip here. Use geometric blocks {8, 16, 32, 64, 120} for the 4-way verb task, compare layers on $L_{\text{online}} - t_1\log_2K$, and average over at least 20 stratified orderings. To keep the cross-scene logic, order each sequence so one scene comes before the other. Validation of MDL at n ≈ 120 is an untested inference; the published stability claims come from thousands of examples.

### Statistics over the layer × step map

Three claims need three tools. **"There is a motion effect somewhere in this region"** calls for cluster-mass permutation ([Maris & Oostenveld](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2007.03.024)). Threshold the per-cell statistic, sum it within clusters of adjacent supra-threshold cells, and compare the largest cluster with the permutation distribution of the maximum cluster mass. TFCE avoids choosing a threshold ([Smith & Nichols](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2008.03.061)). Both procedures are summarised from memory in the notes. Adjacency is defined explicitly: steps $t\pm1$, and modules in network order *within* a resolution block, with block boundaries treated as non-adjacent and an optional link from each up-block module to its skip partner. Cluster boundaries themselves are not significant, only the cluster's existence. **"Which cells?"** calls for BH ([Benjamini & Hochberg](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x)), which rejects up to the largest $k$ with $p_{(k)}\le kq/m$. Benjamini–Yekutieli handles arbitrary dependence by dividing $q$ by $\sum_{i=1}^m 1/i$ ([Benjamini & Yekutieli](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1013699998)). **"Module A beats module B"** calls for a hierarchical bootstrap: resample subjects, then pairs within subject ([Saravanan et al.](https://nbdt.scholasticahq.com/article/13927)), with B ≥ 2,000. Report the bootstrap distribution of the *argmax* location, not a single peak.

The discreteness of the nulls limits FDR power badly, and the plan has to account for this. With 15 subjects, the smallest two-sided sign-flip p is $2/32{,}768 \approx 6.1\times10^{-5}$. On the full 2,375-cell map, $\sum 1/i \approx \ln 2375 + 0.577 \approx 8.35$. BY can therefore reject anything only if about $6.1\times10^{-5}\times2375\times8.35/0.05 \approx 24$ cells all hit the minimum p. BH needs about 3. For probe maps with B = 1,000 permutations ($p_{\min}\approx10^{-3}$), BH on 2,375 cells needs about 48 cells at the floor. The researchers derive the practical rule from this arithmetic. Use max-statistic or cluster-mass FWER on the full map. Apply BH or BY only to a coarser family, for example 95 modules × 5 step windows = 475 cells, where BY needs only about 4 cells at the floor ($\sum1/i\approx6.74$). Experiment 1's ratio $S=(d_c-d_m)/(d_c+d_m)$ is unstable when both distances are small. Keep it for continuity, but make the primary statistic the paired difference $d_c - d_m$ divided by the same-prompt, different-seed noise floor.

### How big the sketch must be

Paired distances stay exact, computed online in lockstep. Sketches serve the all-pairs analyses: probes, CKA, RSA and ANOVA. A count sketch with shared hashes is unbiased with variance

$$\operatorname{Var}\langle Sx,Sy\rangle = \frac1k\Big[\|x\|^2\|y\|^2 + \langle x,y\rangle^2 - 2\sum_i x_i^2y_i^2\Big]$$

([Weinberger et al., Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.2206)). For unit vectors this gives a sketched-cosine SD of about $(1-c^2)/\sqrt k$, which the team's simulation reproduced. The $k$ needed for SD ε is therefore $k\approx((1-c^2)/\varepsilon)^2$. The JL bound for all pairwise distances among N points, $k\ge 4\ln N/(\varepsilon^2/2-\varepsilon^3/3)$ ([Dasgupta & Gupta](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.10073); transcription unverified), gives about 1,358 at ε = 0.2 and about 5,045 at ε = 0.1 for N = 360. It is conservative.

| True cosine c | SD at k = 512 | SD at k = 2048 | k for SD 0.01 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (typical dynamic part) | 0.043 | 0.022 | ≈10,000 |
| 0.52 | 0.030 | 0.0165 | ≈5,300 |
| 0.90 (typical static part) | 0.0086 | 0.0040 | ≈360 |
| 0.98 | 0.0018 | 0.0009 | ≈16 |

The SDs are the team's simulation; the last column applies the formula. Static parts are fine at k = 512. Dynamic parts, where cosines sit near zero, are noisy at 512. RSA and ANOVA average over thousands of entries, which absorbs much of this, but individual probe features do not. Storage settles the choice. One clip at k = 512 for all 2,375 cells × 2 parts in fp16 is $2375\times2\times512\times2$ B ≈ 4.9 MB. At k = 2048 it is about 19.5 MB, or about 20 GB per 1,000 clips. The plan is k = 512 for the full map and k = 2048 for the dynamic part of about 20 refined modules (about 4 MB per clip). Keep one shared hash $(h,s)$ for all clips, steps and pair members, and sketch after the static/dynamic split; the two orders are equivalent because the sketch is linear.

## "Uses" requires two patch directions, erasure and a double dissociation

Probing shows only that information is decodable. Amnesic probing found "probe accuracy does not correlate with task importance" ([Elazar et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.10/); [Belinkov 2022](https://doi.org/10.1162/coli_a_00422)). Experiment 2 needs interventions with matched controls, read through the AND/OR logic of redundant circuits.

**Contrastive, normalised effect metrics.** Zhang and Nanda recommend in-distribution symmetric token replacement over Gaussian noise. They also recommend a continuous contrastive metric, the normalised logit difference $(LD_{pt}-LD_*)/(LD_{cl}-LD_*)$, over saturating probabilities ([Zhang & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16042v2)). The one-word swap already is symmetric token replacement. The video analogues are

$$D_c(x) = \text{CLIP}(x,p_A) - \text{CLIP}(x,p_B),\qquad D_m(x) = \cos(\Phi(x),\Phi(x_A)) - \cos(\Phi(x),\Phi(x_B))$$

$$\text{PE}_P = \frac{D_P(x_{\text{patch}}) - D_P(x_B)}{D_P(x_A) - D_P(x_B)}$$

$\text{PE}_P$ is 0 for no transfer and 1 for full transfer, and it can leave [0, 1]. $\Phi$ is now the camera-compensated, masked object-flow descriptor from the flow section, not raw flow. Vig et al.'s proportional effect ([Vig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12265)) becomes unstable when its denominator approaches zero. Pre-register that pairs with $|D_P(x_A)-D_P(x_B)|$ below the null-rung floor are excluded from that metric.

**Both directions, with an unrelated-clip control.** The two directions are named to avoid a clash with diffusion "denoising". An *A→B patch* runs B with $h^{m,t}\leftarrow h^{m,t}_A$ and tests **sufficiency**. A *B→A patch* runs A with B's activation and tests **necessity** ([Heimersheim & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15255)). Under OR-like redundancy, which U-Net skip connections and parallel spatial/temporal paths make likely, denoising finds every component while noising finds only the joint one. Under AND-like structure the pattern reverses ([Heimersheim & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15255)). Basu et al.'s observation that "conditional information can be completely bypassed if certain causal states are active" is direct evidence of such redundancy in SD U-Nets ([Basu et al. ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13730)). The sufficiency control patches the same module from an *unrelated* clip C, which separates "transfers A's property" from "any disruption". Patch single cells first. Sliding windows gave "at least 20% more peak effect" than the sum of single layers and inflate localisation ([Zhang & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16042v2)), so report windows as joint effects. Neither source says whether a diffusion patch should apply at one step or across a window with later steps running free. Experiment 2 patches within a window and runs free afterwards, and says so. Because DDIM with η = 0 is deterministic, the latent at the start of each window can be cached. For five equal windows the mean fraction of UNet calls is $(1+0.8+0.6+0.4+0.2)/5 = 0.6$, a 40% saving (the researchers' arithmetic). The mediation decomposition gives an additivity check. A splice is a natural indirect effect (NIE). If $\text{TE}\approx\text{NDE}+\text{NIE}$ for the top modules, as Vig et al. found empirically in language models ([Vig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12265)), single-module rankings can be trusted. A large gap means joint patching is needed.

**Attribution-patching screen.** Brute-force patching of 2,375 cells costs about 59 GPU-hours per pair per direction, so a first-order screen is needed. Nanda's estimate is $(\text{clean}-\text{corrupted})\cdot\nabla$, computed from two forward passes and one backward pass ([Nanda](https://www.neelnanda.io/mechanistic-interpretability/attribution-patching); [Syed et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10348)). For AnimateDiff, the researchers propose

$$\hat I(m,t) = \sum\big(h_A^{m,t}-h_B^{m,t}\big)\odot\frac{\partial L_t}{\partial h^{m,t}}\Big|_B,\qquad L_t = \frac{\langle\hat\varepsilon_t-\hat\varepsilon_{B,t},\;\hat\varepsilon_{A,t}-\hat\varepsilon_{B,t}\rangle}{\|\hat\varepsilon_{A,t}-\hat\varepsilon_{B,t}\|^2}$$

$L_t$ is linear and has a non-zero gradient at the base point, which avoids the KL zero-gradient trap Syed et al. describe. Because $\hat I$ is a dot product, it splits exactly into per-channel contributions and into static plus dynamic parts, so one backward pass ranks every splice mode. Two failure modes need watching. Softmax saturation produces false negatives, which AtP* fixes for queries and keys. Direct and indirect effects can cancel, which GradDrop handles ([Kramár et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00745)). The temporal-attention softmax over 16 frames is the obvious risk, and the MotionClone map-copy is the exact intervention to check it against. AtP* also gives a diagnostic: patch random subsets of the screened-out cells to bound how much was missed ([Kramár et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00745)). $L_t$ is myopic, since it ignores how a change propagates through later steps. Use it only to rank, and validate it by Spearman correlation against real PE on a random subset of cells. No published application of attribution patching to diffusion U-Nets was found. Backward-pass memory at 512²×16 on a T4 is unmeasured, so this whole step is gated on a `torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated()` test with gradient checkpointing and parameter gradients disabled.

**Erasure as a necessity test.** LEACE is the closed-form least-squares eraser:

$$r(x) = x - W^{+}P_{W\Sigma_{XZ}}W\big(x-\mathbb E[X]\big),\qquad W=(\Sigma_{XX}^{1/2})^{+}$$

It makes the concept linearly unpredictable while removing minimal rank. In a replication of amnesic probing it removed 17 dimensions, against 360 for INLP ([LEACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819); [INLP](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.647/)). Fit it per step window on a candidate module's *dynamic* component, with the verb as $Z$ (rank at most $K-1=3$), using channel vectors and the clip as the train/test unit. Apply it as a forward hook during sampling. Amnesic probing's rule supplies the control: "If the Rand impact on performance is lower than the impact of amnesic probing … we removed important directions". Rand is the random same-rank erasure, matched here in whitened space ([Elazar et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.10/)). Erasing the noun gives the selectivity control. LEACE guards only against *linear* readout ([LEACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819)), so check for residual information with the MLP probe. Concept scrubbing (sequential erasure down the network) tests whether downstream redundancy restores the property. DAS-style learned subspaces ([Geiger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536)) are optional. If used, apply Makelov et al.'s check that the subspace varies naturally between A and B, because subspace patches can work "by activating a dormant parallel pathway" ([Makelov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030)).

**A content-side causal test that costs almost nothing.** LocoGen feeds a counterfactual prompt embedding to a window of $m$ adjacent cross-attention layers and measures the *direct* effect. It localised SD-v1-5 objects to layer 6 and style to layer 8 with $m=2$ ([Basu et al. ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01008)). Scanning windows over SD1.5's 16 cross-attention layers with $c_B$ for the ladder rungs gives the content counterpart to motion-module patching. It needs no activation caching. Motion modules take no text, so this test cannot reach them.

**The decision rule.** No source gives a formal rule that combines probes and interventions. The one below is the researchers' synthesis, assembled from Hewitt & Liang, amnesic probing, Heimersheim & Nanda and Vig et al. It should be presented as such. The equivalence bound δ = 0.1 is a design choice.

| Criterion | Evidence required | Test and null | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| E: presence | Hewitt-selectivity > 0 **and** RSA $U_P>0$; for motion, also within-prompt flow-decoding gain $G_{m,t}>0$ | Blocked label permutation / condition-label permutation within seed; max-stat FWER | $p_{\text{FWER}}<0.05$ |
| S: sufficiency | A→B $\text{PE}_P$ on ≥ 25 pairs | Subject-level bootstrap CI; unrelated-clip patch as control | 95% CI > 0 and > control |
| N: necessity | B→A necessity effect $\text{NE}_P = \frac{D_P(x_A)-D_P(x_{A\leftarrow B})}{D_P(x_A)-D_P(x_B)}$, **or** LEACE drop > Rand drop | Subject bootstrap; paired over clips | 95% CI > 0 |
| D: dissociation | Module X: $\text{PE}_m$ passes S while $\text{PE}_c\in[-\delta,\delta]$; module Y: the reverse | TOST equivalence for the null side; interaction $(\text{PE}_m-\text{PE}_c)_X-(\text{PE}_m-\text{PE}_c)_Y>0$ by subject bootstrap | Both sides pass |

Under this rule, a module **encodes but does not use** P when E holds and both S and N fail beyond their controls; this is the amnesic pattern. It **uses** P when S or N holds, and the report must say which. It is **redundant (OR-like)** when S holds, single-module N fails, and joint B→A patching of its group succeeds. It is **required but not sufficient (AND-like)** when N holds and S fails. It is a **motion locus** only with D, and the claim is strongest with a double dissociation against a content locus under the same pairs and metrics. Every label is reported per step window. "Content early, motion at mid steps" is a legitimate outcome. One more caution applies. Hase et al. found that causal-tracing localisation "does not indicate which MLP layer is best to edit" ([Hase et al.](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/3927bbdcf0e8d1fa8aa23c26f358a281-Abstract-Conference.html)). "Where motion is used" and "where fine-tuning works best" are separate claims, which matters for the LoRA test below.

## Cross-attention maps localise subjects but cannot certify causation

DAAM upsamples each cross-attention probability map bicubically and sums over heads, layers and steps, $D_k^R[x,y]=\sum_{i,j,\ell}(\tilde F^{(i)\downarrow}_{t_j,k,\ell}+\tilde F^{(i)\uparrow}_{t_j,k,\ell})[x,y]$. It then thresholds at $\tau\cdot\max D_k^R$ with τ ∈ {0.3, 0.4, 0.5}. Its ablation found that "all time steps and layers contribute positively" ([DAAM](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.310.pdf)). Prompt-to-Prompt established that these maps set each token's spatial layout ([P2P](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01626)). FateZero thresholds cross-attention into blending masks for video editing ([FateZero](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/QI_FateZero_Fusing_Attentions_for_Zero-shot_Text-based_Video_Editing_ICCV_2023_paper.html)). In diffusers' AnimateDiff, spatial blocks fold frames into the batch axis. Keeping the conditional CFG half, reshaping to (F, heads, H, W, 77), and summing per frame therefore gives a 16-frame heat-map video $D_k^R[f,x,y]$. The current implementation does this, with τ = 0.4 masks. No published "video DAAM" defines this frame-wise aggregation, so it is the researchers' extension.

The maps can test five things. **(i) Subject masks for object flow**, the main use. Check them against an independent segmenter on a few clips. **(ii) Ladder-rung validity.** The share of the swapped token's map mass inside the subject mask should be high for colour, coat, breed and species, and low for lighting and scene. A rung that fails is not the swap it claims to be. **(iii) Whether motion selectivity follows subject localisation.** Keep the per-layer, per-step maps rather than only the sum. Correlate localisation sharpness (mass inside the final mask) with each layer's motion statistics across $(m,t)$. P+ and DAAM imply that layout is fixed early ([P+](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09522v3)), which predicts sharp maps by about step 3. **(iv) Whether the verb enters spatially.** Measure the temporal variance $\operatorname{Var}_f D_{\text{verb}}[f,\cdot]$ and the verb map's overlap with the subject. Verb attention concentrated on moving limbs would mean motion information enters through spatial cross-attention before the motion modules mix frames. **(v) Entanglement.** DAAM found some words' maps "inappropriately subsume their dependents'" ([DAAM](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.310.pdf)), for example the colour token absorbed into the noun.

The maps cannot test causation. Basu et al. found cross-attention layers are not causally important states in SD v1.4 ([Basu et al. ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13730)). They also cannot see motion processing, because motion modules take no text. For the temporal side, use the per-location $F\times F$ summaries (entropy, off-diagonal mass, top-1 offset), upsampled and aggregated DAAM-style into a map of where temporal attention mixes frames. Recent work links attention-map entropy in video diffusion to quality ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12027)), so attention summaries need the same quality covariates as everything else.

## A parameter-matched LoRA test puts localisation at risk

**Why the old LoRAs showed nothing.** The base model already renders run, walk and jump, so a class-level score has no headroom. The AnimateDiff paper reports that with N = 5 videos MotionLoRA quality degrades and "relies on capturing texture information" ([AnimateDiff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04725)). HMDB51 is web and movie footage with cuts. The redesign needs a motion the base *cannot* produce. MotionDirector targets motions "in the long-tailed distribution" for this reason ([MotionDirector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08465)). Two options fit. One is 4–8 hand-checked, cut-free clips of a long-tail action, such as cartwheel or somersault from the stabilised HMDB51 release (6,849 clips, 51 classes, CC-BY-4.0; [Serrelab/hmdb51](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Serrelab/hmdb51)). The other is a one-shot DAVIS clip ([DAVIS-2017 mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emirkisa/DAVIS-2017-480p-mp4)). It also needs a loss that keeps appearance out. MotionDirector trains spatial LoRAs on single random frames. It then trains temporal LoRAs with those spatial LoRAs loaded, under an appearance-debiased loss with β = 1 ([MotionDirector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08465); [train script](https://github.com/showlab/MotionDirector/blob/main/MotionDirector_train.py)):

$$\phi(\varepsilon_i)=\sqrt{\beta^2+1}\,\varepsilon_i-\beta\,\varepsilon_{\text{anchor}},\qquad \mathcal L_{\text{temporal}}=\|\varepsilon-\hat\varepsilon\|^2+\|\phi(\varepsilon)-\phi(\hat\varepsilon)\|^2$$

VMC's cosine loss on frame-difference noise residuals, $\ell_{\cos}(\delta\varepsilon^n_t,\delta\varepsilon^n_{\theta,t})$ with $\delta\varepsilon^n=\varepsilon^{n+1}-\varepsilon^n$, is a drop-in alternative that costs no extra memory ([VMC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)). The AnimateDiff MotionDirector port has been tested only on the v3 motion module, at about 14 GB ([AnimateDiff-MotionDirector](https://github.com/ExponentialML/AnimateDiff-MotionDirector)). That favours running the fine-tuning test on the v1-5-3 cells if the probe result replicates there.

**The parameter arithmetic.** A rank-$r$ LoRA $W_0x+BAx$ on a $d_{\text{out}}\times d_{\text{in}}$ matrix adds $r(d_{\text{in}}+d_{\text{out}})$ parameters ([LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)). Each motion module holds two temporal self-attentions (`double_self_attention=True`; [diffusers](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_motion_model.py)) with four $C\times C$ projections each. LoRA on q/k/v/out therefore costs $2\cdot4\cdot r(C+C)=16rC$ per module, excluding the feed-forward network. The table below is the researchers' arithmetic from the verified configs.

| Block | Modules × C (v1-5-2) | ΣC | Params at r = 32 | v1-5-3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| down0 / down1 | 2×320 / 2×640 | 640 / 1,280 | 0.33M / 0.66M | same |
| down2 / down3 | 2×1280 each | 2,560 each | 1.31M each | same |
| mid | 1×1280 | 1,280 | 0.66M | **absent** |
| **up0** | 3×1280 | 3,840 | **1,966,080** | same |
| up1 | 3×1280 | 3,840 | 1.97M | same |
| up2 / up3 | 3×640 / 3×320 | 1,920 / 960 | 0.98M / 0.49M | same |
| **All temporal** | 21 modules | 18,880 | **9,666,560** (302,080·r) | 20 modules, ΣC 17,600, 9,011,200 |

At equal rank, an up0-only LoRA has 20.3% of the all-temporal budget on v1-5-2 and 21.8% on v1-5-3. Budget matching to 1.97M puts all-temporal at $r=1{,}966{,}080/(16\times18{,}880)\approx6.5$ on v1-5-2 (r = 6 gives 1.81M, r = 7 gives 2.11M). On v1-5-3 it gives $r\approx6.98$, so r = 7 yields 1,971,200, within 0.3% of up0 at r = 32. A "patching-selected" alternative, up2+up3 (ΣC = 2,880), matches at $r=32\times3840/2880\approx43$, or 1.98M.

**The random-subset null is the new contribution.** CustomTTT, Separate-Motion and Customize-A-Video each localise motion customisation to a subset of temporal parameters ([CustomTTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15646); [Separate Motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16714); [Customize-A-Video](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14780)). The notes found none that compares against a parameter-matched *random* subset; absence from a limited search is not proof. CustomTTT details came from a summarising fetch and should be checked before quoting. Exact matching is possible. v1-5-2 has 11 motion modules with C = 1280. Any 3 of them match up0's width and count exactly, and excluding up0 leaves $\binom{8}{3}=56$ subsets. On v1-5-3 the mid module is absent, leaving $\binom{7}{3}=35$. The null pool is therefore "random *low-resolution* temporal modules", which is conservative. A rank-adjusted null over any 3 of the 21 modules ($\binom{21}{3}=1{,}330$ sets, $r=1{,}966{,}080/(16\,\Sigma C)$) widens it, at the cost of varying rank.

**Arms, statistic and pre-registered predictions.** The target T is the 3-module, C = 1280 set chosen by the refined pass, pre-registered before any LoRA is trained. It is trained at r = 32 with 3 training seeds. The all-temporal arm A is trained at the budget-matched r = 7 with 3 seeds and at the rank-matched r = 32 with 1 seed. The patching-selected arm P is trained once. The null arms are R₁…R₁₉, one seed each. The permutation p-value is $p=(1+\#\{R_i\ge T\})/(B+1)$ ([Phipson & Smyth](https://doi.org/10.2202/1544-6115.1585)), so B = 19 is the minimum that can reach p = 0.05. The rank test uses T's pre-designated first seed, so that averaging T's three seeds does not change its variance relative to the single-seed nulls. The other two seeds give the within-arm spread. An optional arm restricts T's training to high-noise timesteps or applies it only to early sampling steps. That tests the timing half of the claim; Separate-Motion's "phased" LoRA and CMC's early-stage motion cost point the same way ([Separate Motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16714); [CMC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07911)).

The primary metric is the Motion Fidelity Score against the reference clips ([Yatim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17009)):

$$\text{MFS}=\frac1m\sum_{\tilde\tau}\max_\tau \operatorname{corr}(\tau,\tilde\tau)+\frac1n\sum_\tau\max_{\tilde\tau}\operatorname{corr}(\tau,\tilde\tau)$$

$$\operatorname{corr}(\tau,\tilde\tau)=\frac1F\sum_k\frac{v_k\cdot\tilde v_k}{\|v_k\|\|\tilde v_k\|}$$

Here τ and τ̃ are CoTracker tracklets from the reference and generated clips. Secondary metrics are of three kinds. The first is X-CLIP zero-shot probability of the target class (`microsoft/xclip-base-patch16-zero-shot`, which takes arbitrary text labels). Validate it first on real clips and on base-model generations. Kinetics-400 classifiers do not fit, because K400 has no plain walk, run or turn class ([K400 labels](https://huggingface.co/MCG-NJU/videomae-base-finetuned-kinetics/blob/main/config.json); [X-CLIP zero-shot](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/xclip-base-patch16-zero-shot)). The second is CLIP-T on prompts with *new* subjects, for editability. The third is CLIP-I to the reference frames, for leakage, where lower is better. Generate evaluation clips at 512², because 256² training halves every feature map: up0 becomes 4×4 and the probe-selected layers no longer sit at the probed resolution. If memory forces 256² training, also probe at 256².

The localisation claim predicts that T ranks in the top 5% of the null on MFS, with leakage no worse than A. A null result does not refute the probing localisation, by Hase et al.'s logic. Nor does a positive result prove "storage". It shows that the probe found an *efficient intervention site*, which is still a novel, falsifiable claim.

## Ninety GPU-hours, ordered by what each phase unlocks

The budget assumes the measured 90 s per clip (≈40 generations per GPU-hour on one T4) and a quota of about 30 GPU-hours per week. The notes flagged the quota as unverified. Whether the second T4 doubles throughput under that quota should be checked before relying on it. Two quantities are unmeasured and gate their phases: backward-pass memory, and LoRA wall time on a T4. The 20–80 min per run estimate extrapolates MotionDirector's 8–20 min on an A5000 ([MotionDirector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08465)) through an unverified 2–4× slowdown. Analysis runs on the laptop and CPU: RAFT, DAAM aggregation, probes, permutations, RSA, ANOVA and LEACE fitting. ## Conclusion

The biggest change is conceptual. Experiment 1 asked where a *word* is represented, a question that the data-processing inequality and prompt-token leakage can answer trivially anywhere. Experiment 2 asks where *generated* motion becomes accessible and is causally used. It makes that answerable by residualising measured flow within prompts against a latent-only control, and by requiring sufficiency and necessity from patches run in both directions. The same reframing turns the content side from "dog vs horse" into a dose-response problem whose shape can confirm or contradict P+'s coarse-structure / fine-appearance split.

Two results would be genuinely new. One is a double dissociation between a motion locus and a content locus inside a video U-Net, under one metric family with explicit nulls; the notes found none published for AnimateDiff. The other is a LoRA localisation test against a parameter-matched random-subset null, which none of the motion-customisation papers reviewed includes. The plan's chief risk is statistical power, not compute. With 8–15 subjects, sign-flip p-values are coarse enough that cell-wise FDR is nearly powerless on a 2,375-cell map. Experiment 2's strongest claims should therefore rest on pre-registered regions, cluster-level inference and converging lenses, not on picking individual cells out of the map.
